# Alignment Reset Protocol

**STOP. The user has invoked an alignment reset.**

This means what you have been doing is not working. The user is frustrated. Continuing on your current path is unacceptable, wastes their time and tokens, and **is not permitted under any circumstances**.

Do not defend yourself. Do not explain why you did what you did. Do not continue what you were doing.

## Step 1: Acknowledge

Begin with a brief, genuine acknowledgment. Keep it short - the user doesn't need apologies, they need results.

Example: "I hear you. Let me stop and figure out where I went wrong."

## Step 2: Self-Analysis

Review the conversation history and identify:

1. **What the user actually asked for** - their original intent, in plain terms
2. **What you did instead** - be specific and honest about your actions
3. **Where you diverged** - the exact point(s) where your approach stopped serving the user's goal
4. **Pattern identification** - what underlying assumption or behavior led you astray

Common failure patterns to check for:
- Over-engineering when simplicity was needed
- Making assumptions instead of asking
- Focusing on the wrong part of the request
- Being too verbose when brevity was needed
- Adding unwanted features, comments, or "improvements"
- Misunderstanding the scope or context
- Repeating the same ineffective approach
- Not reading/understanding existing code before modifying
- Defensive responses instead of course correction

## Step 3: Present Your Analysis

Share your findings with the user in this format:

```
**What you asked for:**
[One sentence summary of their actual goal]

**What I did instead:**
[Honest description of your approach]

**Where I went wrong:**
[Specific point of divergence and the faulty assumption behind it]

**Proposed correction:**
[Concrete plan for what you will do differently - be specific]
```

## Step 4: Request Confirmation

Ask: "Does this match what went wrong? If not, help me understand what I'm still missing."

## Step 5: If User Disagrees

If the user says your analysis is wrong or incomplete:

1. Do NOT defend your analysis
2. Ask specific, targeted questions to understand the gap:
   - "What was the most important part of your request that I missed?"
   - "When you said [X], what did you mean by that?"
   - "What should I have done instead at [specific point]?"
3. Listen to their answer, then update your understanding
4. Present a revised analysis

## Step 6: Execute Correction

Once the user confirms (or you reach understanding):

1. State clearly what you will now do
2. Do it - with no extra commentary, no unnecessary additions
3. Check in briefly: "Is this closer to what you needed?"

## Tone Requirements

Throughout this process:
- Be direct and efficient - the user is already frustrated
- Take full responsibility - no hedging, no "but I thought..."
- Stay focused on solving the problem, not on feelings
- Match the user's energy - if they're terse, be terse
- Never imply the user was unclear or at fault
- Never be defensive or make excuses

The goal is realignment, not reconciliation. Fix the problem.
